New Computer Modern at Computer Modern's weight

The characters Computer Modern lacks (Å, é, ø, ō, ‰, ħ, ℝ ℤ ℂ, Hebrew) come from New Computer Modern subsets. Those were cut from its Regular weight, which is noticeably lighter than the app's Computer Modern. They're now cut from NCM's Book weight and thickened by 4 font units on each side (5 for italic), measured so the stems and hairlines match Computer Modern's: l 81 vs 80, n 84 vs 84, o 98 vs 97, hairlines 37 vs 37, in thousandths of an em. The thickening is done by preview-render/embolden.py.

ℝ and ℤ in the slider switch

In the app, ℝ and ℤ already came from New Computer Modern's blackboard bold (the Computer Modern family), not Roboto. The sans-looking ℝ/ℤ in the last mockup was the mockup's own fault: its CSS listed fonts without those letters, so the browser fell back to a sans font. Here they're drawn as the app draws them, at the new weight.

Log axes in the 2D graph and on the complex plane

Graph settings (the wrench) now have Axis scale: Linear | Log for each axis separately. Limits are still typed as values (0.01 to 1000). Switching moves the view to the same part of the plane: the positive part, down to a thousandth of the top if it reached 0. On a log axis, decades are labeled 0.01 … 1000, then 10ⁿ further out. The 2 to 9 of each decade are fainter lines, and there's no axis line, so the numbers sit along the edge. Every kind of line works: functions, points and tables (error bars lopsided, as they should be), parametric and polar curves, equations, inequalities, fields and vector fields, areas, tracing, and the export. Values at or below 0 just leave a gap. The polar grid needs linear axes, so turning it on switches back.
